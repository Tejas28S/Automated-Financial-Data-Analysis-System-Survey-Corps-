The structural pattern: a transaction's date is split across a page boundary — DD-MM- prints at the foot of page N, the year 2024 prints at the top of page N+1, separated by the full page header. DATE_AT_LINE_START_PATTERN requires a complete date, so the page-bottom row (10-05- … 2,000.00 2,017.00) isn't recognized as a transaction and is dropped. That's why the next real row (SentIMPS … balance 17) fails: its true predecessor balance 2017 never existed in the parse. One dropped row per page boundary ≈ the 17 failing rows.

My earlier Issue-1 fix handled the metadata-block interruption sub-case; this is the split-date sub-case it doesn't cover.

Why the proposed solution generalizes (and avoids overfitting)
The fix keys on a document-structural signature, never on any value:

Trigger: a line beginning with a date that is missing its year — day-month with a trailing separator and no following digit (^\d{1,2}[/\-]\d{1,2}[/\-](?!\d)). A complete date (10-05-2024…) has a digit after the 2nd separator, so it can never match. This partial form essentially only occurs when a date is physically truncated at a boundary.
Repair: borrow the carried-over year from the next leading-year fragment (^(\d{4}|\d{2})\b, validated as a plausible year) found before the next real transaction line, splice it onto the truncated date, and strip it from the fragment.
Guards: bounded forward window; stop at the next date-started line (never steal a year across a real transaction); reject 3-digit/non-year leading numbers (so an address like 546 52 … is skipped); do nothing if the pattern is absent.
It's bank-agnostic (no filename/bank/IFSC/holder), and it solves the whole page-split-date class — the same one the contract's 27-03- / 2024 example describes. Let me implement it.
